# Content Storytelling

Identify the audience's central question and the understanding or decision the artifact should support.
Choose an arc that fits: question -> evidence -> answer, current state -> workflow -> application, or problem -> options -> recommendation.
Do not impose a sales pitch on a neutral technical reference.

- Give each section or slide a distinct job; remove duplicated explanations and generic claims.
- Lead with the important finding, then show the evidence and its practical meaning.
- Keep definitions, periods, currencies and entity names consistent across formats.
- Explain a feature through a useful scenario: question, inputs, method, answer and limitation.
- Distinguish supported implementation, live verified results, data-dependent features and proposed work.
- Place full inventories in appendices while preserving the context needed to interpret them.
- Close with a decision, application or next action that follows from the evidence.

For visual production, compose with `professional-artifact-design` and the installed format skill.
Do not invent quotations, screenshots, business results or causal explanations to strengthen a story.
